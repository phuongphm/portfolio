The image of family is often associated with shared meals, conversations after a long day, or simply the comforting feeling of coming home. Family is where we find love, trust, and a sense of belonging.

But in today's technology-driven world, where the ways we learn, communicate, and consume information are constantly evolving, I began to wonder: Is the concept of family evolving alongside these changes?

Family should not only be a place to return to, but also a space where every member can share their world, including their digital one. This is the core value behind FAMIL.AI: creating a space where parents and children can explore, learn, and better understand one another in the age of AI.

## A Small Gap Between Two Generations

The idea for FAMIL.AI began during a briefing session for the UNESCO Youth Hackathon. One issue particularly caught my attention: many parents may not know how their children are using AI.

As a programming teacher, I immediately thought of my students. When I asked them about their use of AI, I received some surprising answers. Some used it to solve homework problems or search for information, while others shared that they talked to AI and even confided personal matters in it.

I realized that AI was no longer an unfamiliar concept to children. It had gradually become part of how they learned, entertained themselves, and interacted with the world.

That made me wonder: Do parents understand how their children interact with AI? Do they know where the information their children encounter online comes from or what risks they might face?

I realized that the issue was not simply about children using technology too much or parents lacking technological knowledge. Perhaps what was missing was an opportunity for both generations to sit down, share their experiences, and understand each other's digital worlds.

And that was when an idea came to me: **What if we created a platform where parents and children could learn about AI, Media and Information Literacy (MIL), and online safety together?**

## From a Small Idea to FAMIL.AI

After the briefing session, I started looking for teammates through a Facebook group. Eventually, our team came together with four members, each bringing different strengths and responsibilities.

I initiated the idea and coordinated the overall direction of the project, while other members focused on research, communications, and proposal development.

I named the project **FAMIL.AI**, combining three key elements: Family, Media and Information Literacy (MIL), and Artificial Intelligence (AI).

Rather than creating a platform that simply delivered information or advice about online safety, I wanted to make learning more engaging for both parents and children.

That was why we decided to develop FAMIL.AI as an interactive web-based game.

## Learning Together Instead of Just Teaching Each Other

One of the aspects I love most about FAMIL.AI is how it encourages interaction between parents and children through role reversal.

In the game, two family members join the same room using a Room Code and receive a shared scenario. One takes on the role of the **Media Composer**, who creates media content, while the other becomes the **Investigator**, responsible for analyzing and evaluating it.

The Media Composer combines different elements, such as images, headlines, captions, and contextual information, to create a seemingly credible social media post. The Investigator must observe, ask questions, and decide whether the content is trustworthy.

![FAMIL.AI iamge](/portfolio/images/blog/familai/FAMILAI.png)

After each round, they switch roles and experience a new scenario.

I particularly love this feature because it challenges the traditional one-way approach to learning, where parents are expected to teach and children are expected to listen. With FAMIL.AI, both generations have the opportunity to become content creators and information evaluators.

An AI facilitator then helps analyze each scenario using the S.P.O.T. Framework, explaining how images, language, emotions, and context may influence our decisions.

For me, the goal is not simply to determine who has the right answer. A parent and a child may interpret the same information differently. Instead of treating these differences as mistakes, I wanted to turn them into opportunities for meaningful conversations.

Because sometimes, what matters most is not whether we arrive at the same answer, but whether we can understand each other's perspectives.

## My First Experience Turning an Idea into a Technology Product

Having an idea is one thing, but turning it into a product that others can interact with is an entirely different challenge.

To build our initial prototype, I decided to use Google AI Studio. This was my first time exploring the tool to develop a web-based product.

I was surprised by how much AI could help me accomplish. By describing my requirements and refining my prompts, I gradually created an interactive interface.

Of course, things did not go smoothly at first. The system sometimes struggled to combine different content elements into a complete social media post or follow the intended game logic.

Instead of giving up, I experimented with different prompts, added more detailed instructions, and generated new versions. I also took the opportunity to learn more about prompt writing and how to communicate with AI more effectively.

This experience taught me that creating a technology product does not necessarily require knowing everything from the beginning. What matters is understanding the problem, finding the right tools, and being willing to learn along the way.

Although our prototype still needs improvement and has not yet been tested with real users, seeing our idea gradually come to life was already a rewarding experience.

## Beyond FAMIL.AI: What I Learned Along the Way

Looking back, FAMIL.AI was much more than a technology project. It was also my first experience leading a team with different backgrounds and areas of expertise.

I had the opportunity to initiate an idea, coordinate the team, design the game, and develop an initial prototype. Through this process, I learned that leadership does not mean doing everything yourself. Sometimes, it means setting a direction, bringing people together, and creating opportunities for everyone to contribute their strengths.

Our team eventually completed the project proposal and video pitch and submitted them to the UNESCO Youth Hackathon. Unfortunately, FAMIL.AI was not selected for the next round, so we did not get the opportunity to present our idea to the judging panel.

![certificate iamge](/portfolio/images/blog/familai/certificate.png)

Although we did not achieve the competition outcome we had hoped for, I am still proud of what we created together. If I had another opportunity, I would love to test the game with real families and continue improving the user experience.

More importantly, this project helped me see Educational Technology (EdTech) from a different perspective. As a programming teacher, I have always been interested in how technology can make learning more engaging and meaningful. Through FAMIL.AI, I discovered that I could do more than use technology in teaching. I could also create learning experiences and develop educational solutions with it.

Perhaps the biggest lesson I learned is that **you don't have to wait until you feel completely ready to start something new.**

You do not have to be an expert or possess every necessary skill. Sometimes, all you need is the courage to begin, people who share your vision, and the willingness to keep learning.

FAMIL.AI started with a small question, but it gave me much more than I had expected: an opportunity to challenge myself, discover new possibilities, and gain a clearer understanding of the path I want to pursue.

And perhaps, this is only the beginning.

*One idea, one experiment, and one step at a time.*
