Recently, I had the opportunity to join the **Aspire Leaders Program (ALP)**, a leadership development program for young adults. During the program, I practiced identifying problems, looking for solutions, and anticipating outcomes through real-world assignments.

For **Assignment Week 4**, instead of choosing a community issue, I decided to start with my own story. It was a question I had been thinking about since I began teaching: **Should I keep gaining practical teaching experience, or invest my time in pursuing a master's degree?**

On one hand, I want more time in the classroom to understand my students and strengthen my teaching practice. On the other, I hope to pursue further study to broaden my knowledge and prepare for long-term career goals. Both matter, but which path is right for me at this point?

## When a personal question may resonate with others

At first, I thought this was only a personal decision. But looking more broadly, I realized that questions about career development may also arise for many early-career teachers.

Some may want to continue their studies but not know where to begin preparing. Others may want to keep teaching and gaining experience, but have not yet mapped out the next steps in their professional development.

That encouraged me to look at the question more systematically instead of weighing two options based only on how I felt.

## From a question to a search for solutions

After defining the issue, I started brainstorming and asking more specific questions. If I continue teaching, what can I do to develop professionally? If I want to pursue a master's degree, what should I prepare to meet the entry requirements of different programs?

Rather than treating these as two paths that require a trade-off, I looked for a way to combine them: continue gaining teaching experience while preparing my language skills, academic profile, and other requirements for future master's applications.

Through this process, I realized that I may not need to make one major decision right away. What matters is building a flexible plan that allows me to grow in my current work while preparing for future learning opportunities.

## What I learned from Assignment Week 4

In the past, when something made me uncertain, I tended to think a lot about the final outcome without breaking the problem down and exploring possible solutions.

This assignment helped me practice a more systematic approach: define the problem, understand the factors involved, brainstorm possible solutions, and consider their potential outcomes. I learned that there is not always one single right answer. Sometimes, the important thing is to find the next step that best fits the current situation and adjust as we gain more experience.

I also realized that leadership does not have to begin with leading a large group. It can start with recognizing a problem, taking responsibility for our choices, and gradually looking for ways to improve the situation.

## A question I am still exploring

For now, I still do not have a final answer about when I should pursue a master's degree. But I no longer see gaining experience and continuing my education as opposing paths.

In the meantime, I will keep learning through my teaching work, explore master's programs, and prepare the requirements I may need to apply in the future.

Career development may not always come down to one big decision made at a single moment. It can also be a process of preparing, trying things out, and adjusting our direction through each experience.

Sometimes, searching for an answer for ourselves is also an opportunity to better understand the kind of teacher we hope to become.

*Learning, unlearning, and growing — one step at a time.*
